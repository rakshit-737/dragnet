Conventions. A baseline *commits* to an actor only when that actor is its unique top score (a tie at the top is an abstention); 'wrong if ties broken at random' scores the alternative convention in expectation. DRAGNET commits at MEDIUM or HIGH; 'wrong actor named (any grade)' also counts its LOW verdicts. Ranking metrics score ties in expectation.

## G - comparison with Guru, Moss & Kochenderfer (2025)

Guru et al. (arXiv:2505.11547) attribute threat reports to 29 actors by scoring P(technique | actor), estimated from technique counts in training reports, against the techniques extracted from a test report (machine extraction with text-embedding-3-large over 727 reports); the metric is the mean rank of the true actor among the 29 (random = 15). Their report corpus and extracted technique counts are not public in reusable form, so the paper's numbers cannot be reproduced exactly. Our adaptation keeps their scorer, actor set, 70/20/10 split per actor and 10 repetitions, but uses ATT&CK's human-curated per-report technique lists (261 reports). Human technique lists are cleaner than machine extraction, so the adaptation is an optimistic upper bound for their pipeline. DRAGNET and the other methods use graphs from which every validation/test report has been removed.
